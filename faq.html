Frequently Asked Questions

Transcription Factors (TFs) are proteins that control the rate of transcription of genetic information from DNA to messenger RNA, by binding to specific DNA sequences. They are essential for regulating gene expression and are often implicated in diseases like cancer.

The Lung Cancer Transcription Factor Database (Lung TFDB) is a comprehensive, free-to-use resource for researchers. It provides a centralized platform for accessing and analyzing a wide range of data related to TFs in lung cancer, including their mutations, expression, and impact on patient survival.

Currently, our database primarily focuses on the two most common types of Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC): Lung Adenocarcinoma (LUAD) and Lung Squamous Cell Carcinoma (LUSC), using data from large-scale projects like The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA).

You can search for a specific gene by entering its official HUGO gene symbol in the search box available in sections like "Protein Sequence", "Mutations," "Expression," and "Survival." Our system supports autocomplete to help you find the gene you are looking for.
